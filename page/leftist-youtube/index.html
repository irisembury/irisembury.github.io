--- title: Thoughts on Leftist YouTube date: 2026-10-07 mirrors: substack:219340446 --- Earlier this year I spent a few weeks watching some popular left-wing YouTube channels that I wasn't familiar with. I don't typically watch this type of stuff, so I'm piecing together the ecosystem still. The four that I ended up focusing on were [Vaush](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC6SY6qoT8wVWeHV9ijeCiDQ) (Ian Kochinski), [Secular Talk](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCldfgbzNILYZA4dmDt4Cd6A) (Kyle Kulinski), the [Majority Report](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC-3jIAlnQmbbVMV6gR7K8aQ) (Sam Seder, Emma Vigeland), and the [Young Turks](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC8Ap0a-VRZALdStTdipHGuA) (Cenk Uygur, Ana Kasparian). There were a few other channels that looked similar (like one called [Breaking Points](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCDRIjKy6eZOvKtOELtTdeUA)), but I decided to stop at these. I watched multiple videos from each channel every day with the goal of becoming familiar with each personality and their views and politics. First, of these, the worst by far was Vaush. There's something especially vile about him compared with the others, both in form and content. It's amplified by how he lacks even feigned professionalism, or that he makes less of an effort to conceal his radicalism. The others are at least trying for some air of legitimacy that Vaush has long since given up on. Still, they all have similar flaws, and, in the big picture, Vaush is worse than the others only superficially. It wouldn't be enough to say these people are dishonest ideologues, or that half of them are just plain stupid, or that they constantly peddle in conspiracy theorism and misinformation, or that they present everything they talk about in an extremely biased manner. That's all true, of course, but these descriptors are common enough online that it wouldn't distinguish them from, say, Patrick Bet-David or Tim Pool. No, the qualities that make these channels different from the rest of the internet, and what groups them together, are less common. Here, I'll explore some of those themes. ## 1: They hate the Democrats (from the left) They obviously all have an axe to grind with the Democratic Party. This comes out in the form of commentary oozing with resentment and bias against the Democrats in a way that might be confusing for a casual viewer, considering it's the party these channels are most aligned with. This could be confused with somebody who supports the party but who's disappointed with it at times when it fails to achieve its goals or win elections, but alas, that's not what's happening. They're not disappointed that it's not achieving its goals, they hate it for what its goals were to begin with. They hate what it is fundamentally, and want it to be something else altogether --- something much further left. In the meantime they hate its leaders past and present, they hate its popular figures, its voters, its policy stances on most topics. They think it's less evil than the Republicans, but not by much. The main attack they engage in is relentlessly implying that Democrats aren't so different from Trump. Kulinski, for example, regularly calls Democratic politicians [MAGA lite](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DXMpebvf6OU) or [Diet MAGA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6wlDurajmkg). That's how you might expect somebody to describe Marco Rubio, who represents a less crass version of the Republican Party compared with the vulgarity of Trump himself. And even if it were hyperbolic, you may understand his frustration if he just used this term once or twice to describe somebody like Fetterman, who's been playing into Republican talking points lately. But no, this has become Kulinski's regular name for Democrats in general, for the likes of Schumer, Harris, Biden, Clinton, or Slotkin. It's not a thoughtful nor truthful analysis of anything: Kulinski just realized he could label anybody he likes with "MAGA" to great rhetorical effect, so he leaned into it. In one video, Kulinski condemns Hillary Clinton and [describes her as going full MAGA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nUm-7gK4QMo). That description might have made sense if he were talking about somebody like Tulsi Gabbard, who used to be a Democrat but [switched parties in 2024](https://thehill.com/homenews/campaign/4948241-tulsi-gabbard-joining-gop-trump-rally-in-north-carolina/), choosing to stand with the Republicans and later becoming part of Trump's government. But Hillary Clinton? That's one of the most stalwart left-wing politicians in the country. What did she do wrong to be so strongly condemned? She expressed concern about how there's a lot of misinformation on social media, and some young people are forming strong opinions about topics they know very little about. Isn't this practically the opposite of the Trumpian attitude? Kulinski may employ equivocation like this most gratuitously, but he's not alone. Vaush regularly uses [the term uniparty](https://youtu.be/qzT69JXaBYQ?t=1900), a piece of conspiracy theorist jargon meant to imply both major parties are acting together to serve the same agenda. The appearance of them being two, competing parties is merely a sham, an illusion. Ana Kasparian [says it too](https://youtu.be/-_tlr3u2iP0?t=814), asking how the two parties are even different before concluding "on the issues that matter," they're not. The overall message of these channels is that Republicans are bad, but Democrats are only slightly better, so you should hate them too. (As though trying to produce material for this write-up, Vaush even once uploaded a video called [You Don't Hate The Democrats Enough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TGpGC93LCjU).) Over at the Majority Report, they're less on the nose about the equivocation --- they don't use terms like "diet MAGA" nor "uniparty" --- but they're perpetually disappointed by "corporate Democrats," occasionally comment about people being "collaborators," and imply Democrats want to merely exist as the official opposition but without ever actually doing anything, thus forwarding the same sort of mindset, providing another angle to seep into the minds of their overlapping audience. ## 2: They're passionately antisemitic The politics of all these channels has become unreasonably fixated on Israel, Zionists, AIPAC, or really anything connected with Israel or the Jews, as these categories all blur together in the popular consciousness. (AIPAC, the American--Israel Public Affairs Committee, is often confused for a [political action committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_action_committee) due to its name, and often deliberately mischaracterized as though an arm of the Israeli government. It's an organization of Americans who advocate for their government to maintain friendly relations with Israel, supposedly receiving donations from many American Jews.) It goes far beyond a critique of the Israeli government's actions, which many normal people agree with. The common sentiment here is that the Israeli population is so irredeemably bad that the situation could not be resolved except through Israel's total destruction. They believe that Jews cannot be allowed to exist except as a political minority, ideally in an Arab-majority state --- and anybody who disagrees with that mission is called a Zionist, which they hold to be a political slur akin to being a fascist. Jewish people who live in the U.S. are thus implicated and vilified. The political issue of Israel may have been how it began, but at this point they've devolved into paranoid antisemites. Vaush refers to Israel [as a demon country](https://redd.it/1oyyxnn/) and says if someone so much as mentions Israel, the correct response is to spit on the ground. Kulinski regularly denounces people as "Israel first," another term lifted from the far-right, which combined with other themes allows them to effectively reconstruct the neo-Nazi concept of ZOG, or Zionist occupation governments, meaning those countries whose politicians are merely puppets of the world Jewry. Kulinski says European countries are [vassal states of the U.S. who will go along with Israel no matter what](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vFmjHbOnXZE). He's said the U.S. government [is occupied by Israel](https://youtu.be/S2WCUKcEUVk?t=798). Ana Kasparian uses the same language, claiming the U.S. [is occupied and controlled by the Israelis](https://x.com/AnaKasparian/status/1954256169092182162). Cenk Uygur, the most far-gone, skips the dog whistle and directly says [Israel controls America](https://youtu.be/GQq6nvrfvQ8?t=272). In that same rant, he says Jewish culture is an oppressor culture, indifferent to other people's pain, and all they do is lie. Elsewhere he reacts to the U.S. attacking other countries by asking why [Americans have to fight Israel's wars](https://x.com/cenkuygur/status/2007206016581284347). It's not a coincidence Uygur and Kasparian have both [become 9/11 conspiracy theorists](https://x.com/cenkuygur/status/2022343543705252210). The worst, most indefensible display came after a number of Democratic lawmakers condemned Uygur's nephew, Hasan Piker, for his antisemitic remarks in August 2026. Uygur claimed all these politicians (including Slotkin, Ossoff, and Jeffries) were [following orders given by their Israeli handlers from Tel Aviv](https://youtu.be/-_tlr3u2iP0?t=555). Kasparian agreed. ## 3: They hide their power level With the exception of Vaush, I believe these channels all conceal just how far left they are. That alone explains much of their disposition and political incentives. Communists and other far-left radicals present as left-leaning but spend most of their time attacking the moderates, because their goal isn't beating the right, it's taking over the left side. To combat the right only helps left moderates. It can be tricky to say how extreme somebody is when their channel focuses on news coverage, not laying out and enumerating their ideals for you to judge them by. Instead it seeps out over time. And even the people who seem more mild, like Seder, make their alignment clear [when they lie to cover for the extremists](https://x.com/mehdirhasan/status/2087009758842589665) on their side. They attempt to portray theirselves as representing the left-wing base of the party, trying to save us from "centrists" pulling the party rightward. To sell that framing, they must deceive you into overlooking all of the actual left-wing voices in the party, which they label the establishment Dems, the corporate Dems, the centrist Dems, or some other such nebulous phrase meant to be a dig at their progressive credentials without having to specify anything. It's understandably difficult for the average person to intuit just how far left these channels are if they've been fed a lie as outrageous as that Kamala Harris is actually a centrist. It might seem like an exaggeration that I would even mention Communism, as these people don't exactly have Soviet iconography on the wall, but I've not seen or heard anything to rule it out. The modern radical doesn't need to reach into history and defend past instances of their ideology. Why give the game away? Just as well, not everybody we today consider far-right is going to bring up the Nazis or the Confederacy, say. They may occasionally slip up and show signs of such sympathies, but it's not necessary for modern politicking. There is a sort of legacy leftist who does explicitly try and whitewash left-wing dictators like Lenin, Stalin, or Mao, but the more modern sort of leftist will never criticize them for doing so. And their behaviour is generally the same: trying to undermine Western liberal democracy however possible, while casting its enemies (in the past the USSR, today Russia, China, Iran, and Islamic terror groups like Hamas or Hezbollah) in the most sympathetic light possible. No Soviet flag on the wall, but functionally not much different. Both groups end up obsessed with Israel, billionaires, and imperialism, but only because, and only insofar as, these complaints allow them to attack and undermine democratic countries. They don't care about billionaires in China, or imperialism by Russia, or war crimes by Hezbollah. Each faux concern is only a rhetorical weapon against the West. If some of them were secretly Communists and some not, you couldn't tell the difference. ## 4: They're cynical and disillusioned It's possible these people were radicalized by slowly becoming more cynical and disillusioned about political process. Cenk Uygur has been reading headlines into his YouTube channel for over 20 years now, and the others aren't too far behind. Where has it gotten them? They're more popular now than ever, but they're still in the same rooms, making the same arguments, their ideas are still unpopular, their preferred candidates are still not winning. Cenk Uygur tried [getting into real-world politics at some point](https://www.politico.com/states/california/story/2019/11/14/progressive-uygur-seeks-hill-seat-blurs-picture-for-democrats-1226847), but lost and accomplished nothing. How long can you try without success before you start to feel discouraged? These people are also all hyper-online (perhaps defensibly so, as it is their business). They live inside of social media feeds, which present a curated version of reality, an alternate timeline where it feels like everybody agrees and sides with them. Everybody who's not MAGA, that is. So why aren't they in control of the Democratic Party? There's a disconnect between expectation and reality that demands answers. It makes them feel like something isn't right. The tables are tilted, the game is rigged. They turn to the internet for answers, and the internet provides conspiracy theorism as a coping mechanism. Why aren't you getting what you want? Don't look inward. It can't be your ideas. No, *the people* agree with you --- see? Just look at all those comments on your social media! Yes, it must be the system that's borken. It must be the donors, the lobbyists, the consultants, the banks, the Jews. Indeed, the leftists fall for all the same tropes that infected their friends on the right. A major contributor to this kind of disillusionment is the folk belief that Bernie Sanders truly ought to have won the party's nomination for president in 2016, and possibly again in 2020, but the honour was unfairly cheated away from him. It's a myth, a work of fiction believed only by people who have never bothered to look into it seriously, but it's been persistent and helped radicalize many people against the party, pulling them into the world of online grievance politics and populist conspiracy thinking. If you're a normal person, you may not have noticed how much outright conspiracy theorism about the Democratic Party itself has become the norm in left-wing spaces. A common belief is that Democratic leaders deliberately sabotage their own popular agenda to serve the interests of donors. When Lieberman blocked the public option in 2009, or when Manchin and Sinema made the Democrats water down their ambitious 2021 budget, the far-left interpretation is that Democratic leaders like Obama or Pelosi were actually against those goals being achieved all along. They merely appoint certain people to be the "rotating villains" of the party, meaning fall guys who sabotage the party's agenda on purpose, taking the blame for everybody else. It's not a theory supported by evidence nor reason, but if you simply assume it to be true, it can be enough to radicalize a gullible person against the establishment. Cenk Uygur went through a phase of arguing that any instance where the Democratic Party succeeded in passing legislation that benefits people's lives was merely their [activating a pressure relief valve](https://youtu.be/gfzzXXfGKmA?t=160), allowing people a pittance only to keep them from rebellion against the state. Though I don't assume he's capable of such big-picture thinking, one gets the impression he's trying to make room for a Marxist revolutionary narrative wherein all politics is framed around the threat of proletarian revolution staved off by capital --- one in which reform is actually bad, because it satisfies people enough to keep them from resorting to revolution. Either way, he's promoting a completely made-up conspiracy theory that's meant to demotivate people and cause them to disengage from traditional politics. ## 5: They learned from the right YouTube and Twitter, the main platforms where these people reside, are dominated by right-wing shows, personalities, and political culture. Part of how the right wing operates is to always be on the attack. They ensure the spotlight is on Democrats, who always have to answer for and defend everything they do, even while Republicans are in the middle of doing far worse. They've also constructed a canon of stereotypes and just-so stories in the form of memes and stock characters, creating a sort of alternate reality disguised as humour. Instead of trying to break the delusion and deconstruct the right's culture, the leftist largely accepts its premises and tries to make a home for theirselves within it, emulating the right in strategy and posture. Instead of stepping into the spotlight to defend the Democrats, they shrug and say "I hate them too." Then they sit back and join in mocking the Democrats, often repurposing right-wing attacks against them with little to no modification. From that position, the right mostly ignores them, and the Democratic Party mostly ignores them too because their efforts are better spent fighting the Republicans. Thus the far-left ecosystem exists in a balance with the right, and is accustomed to getting little to no serious pushback. They operate as a vicious peanut gallery. !images 300 src:media/spotlight.png |caption: Even now, as the Republicans control the American government, both chambers of congress, the supreme court, most state governments, most of the internet and an increasingly large part of traditional media, and as the Democratic Party is struggling to regain control on all fronts, the far-left continues their anti-Democratic assault. They continue disparaging Democratic leaders and comparing them with MAGA. They tell their audience it's a uniparty, the Democrats don't care about you, they're paid off by Israel and the corporations. The only politicians they express support for are the most fringe left-wing ones who kowtow to them. All in all, the only function these people serve in American politics is making the Democrats' job harder. ## 6: They operate as a third party The U.S. is effectively a two-party system in that it doesn't have any third parties holding office anywhere. It's Republican and Democrats, with a few independents, all the way down. To understand the real third party dynamic, let's look to a nearby parliamentary system whose party names are easy to follow: Canada. In the Canadian House of Commons, there's a Liberal Party and a Conservative Party, representing a standard centre-left and centre-right politics respectively. These two make up the majority of seats, but smaller parties also exist, such as the New Democrats (NDP), whose identity is to be a more left-wing alternative to the Liberals. Consider: what incentives guide this party's behaviour? If the New Democrats wanted to gain members, it clearly makes more sense to appeal to Liberal voters than Conservative ones. It also wouldn't benefit the NDP directly to persuade Conservative voters to move their views left, because they would likely end up voting Liberal, not coming all the way around to the other side. The NDP therefore predictably spends most of its time criticizing and trying to undermine the Liberal Party despite it being the one they're closer to. This is rational, and a small right-wing party would inevitably end up acting the same way toward the Conservative Party. If they didn't spend a significant amount of time distinguishing theirselves from the Liberals, their party would probably fade away. In a first-past-the-post system, voting for a small party on the left benefits the largest party on the right and vice versa. This is called splitting the vote. If you vote for the NDP candidate in your riding, you make it more likely the Conservative wins that seat by overtaking the Liberal candidate. If instead the NDP does win that seat, you're making it more likely the Conservative Party gains control of government by overtaking the Liberals in the House by denying that seat from the Liberals. This increased risk of losing to the right is exactly how a small party like the NDP can influence the country: by making the Liberals feel the need to satisfy their most left-wing voters, lest they lose them to the NDP. And if the Liberals end up as a minority government (the largest party but not a majority, as they were in 2019--2025), the NDP can offer to support their legislation in exchange for policy concessions. They do aspire to someday overtake the Liberals and become the largest left-wing party (this happened briefly in 2011--2015, the first time in Canadian history the Liberals weren't either the largest or second-largest party, falling to third), but this is the risky game they play in the meantime. In the U.S., despite formally being part of the Democrats, the far left is operating like a third party. They conceptualize a faction within the Democratic Party and focus not on the whole left-of-centre winning elections, but on trying to grow their faction within the left. And the best way to do so isn't pulling Republicans leftward, but pulling regular Democratic voters even further left. They may hope to take over the party someday, but until then they can exercise power by forcing policy concessions from Democratic moderates by threatening to not support the party, or by getting just a few candidates elected and holding up legislation in the event of close votes. This behaviour does make a Republican victory more likely, both electorally and policy-wise, but it's the most rational course of action if your primary goal is growing the left faction. Viewed this way, their behaviour is more understandable, albeit still frustrating. How things can play out this way, instead of the far left having to make its own party, seems owing to a quirk of the American political system: there's no mechanism by which national leadership can expel or exclude anybody from membership in the party. If enough people in NY-12 register as Democrats and vote for a person, they get to put their name on the ballot as a Democrat and will be referred to as such once in office, regardless of who they are or what they believe. Other members could personally disavow them, but not de-party them. (In most other democracies, party leaders can directly expel somebody from party membership at any time, removing their party titles and rendering them an independent, and this isn't a rare occurrence. A party therefore cannot be hijacked by an oppositional faction.) There's room for disagreement within a party while still being allies, because you're not competing for votes, the most essential political resource. There are Democratic politicians considered to be firmly on the left side of the party, like Elizabeth Warren. A more centrist Democrat could sit down with Warren and have a collaborative relationship because they're aligned in real terms: they both want you to vote Democrat and for the Democrats to win. If, on the other hand, they were to invite on somebody from the Green Party, the relationship is necessarily antagonistic because they want to persuade people to vote for their candidates instead of the Democrats, and the Democrat must therefore push back against that. And here is where I propose what is not obvious to enough people: the far left that does not generally support the party is effectively acting like a third party, and should therefore be treated as such. Their goal will be to promote their own specific handful of candidates, but muddy the waters to depress turnout where the rest of the party is concerned, even if to the Republicans' advantage. They need to be treated like outsiders and vandals, not teammates, because that's how they think about their own position and it's how they operate and relate to the rest of us.
