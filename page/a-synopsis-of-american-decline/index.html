I don't need to worry much about whether the food or drugs on the shelves are safe. I can buy things with confidence because I know I'm within a system that has rules and regulators every step of the way to ensure this is the case. Imagine what it would be like if all our regulators and quality assurance disappeared, new stores opened up selling things that hadn't been tested, and the possibility of food or drugs at the market being harmful to you skyrockets. What would be the result? If we couldn't implement regulation of some kind, consumers would need to change their behaviour. We would need to become more skeptical shoppers, doing more work to distinguish good from bad product, or to find reputable vendors. We could no longer be as free or careless as we once were. A similar thing happened to our media. It used to be that if you heard a claim on the news or read it in an article by a reputable paper, you could safely assume it was true. They would never fabricate things, they probably made a reasonable attempt to verify it, and they probably even made an effort to look into the topic a bit to present it fairly, instead of uncritically blurting out the first claim they came across. This is still true today when interacting with traditional, reputable media companies. But when the internet started displacing traditional media as the way people got their news, the quality of the product changed. The standards dropped. There were suddenly many more sources of information, without the experience nor the same checks on what they were putting out. So, in response, people need to change their behaviour. We had to become more media literate, more skeptical viewers, doing more work to distinguish good from bad reporting, to find reputable outlets and writers and commentators. But this didn't happen, at least not for enough of us. In general society has failed to adapt to these changes circumstances. Too many people still automatically believe what they see as though it were a trustworthy article by a reputable source, even if it's just some random person with a YouTube channel or an image of text you scrolled by on Facebook or Twitter. It's like we're living through a transitional age where everybody is constantly getting sick from their food and drugs because we're still buying things from the store carelessly as though it were still well-regulated, when it no longer is. There's been an internet media revolution, and it's ruined people's ability to navigate and understand the world around them. A lesson to be learned from the last decade or two is that the fabled "marketplace of ideas" doesn't work as intended, that is, it doesn't work through free association alone, without institutions and rules and enforcement.
