!meta title:Thoughts on Epstein In 2006, Jeffrey Epstein, age 53, was arrested in Florida for paying to have sexual relations with underage girls as young as 14. There were many other girls observed coming to his apartment, some as young as 13. He was convicted in 2008, then released after a plea agreement. In 2019, age 66, he was arrested again for charges relating to trafficking minors for sexual abuse. A month later he committed suicide in his cell at a detention facility in New York City. It was popular online to insist he was murdered. Now years later many of his personal files have been released to the public, and we're able to look back and piece together what he'd been up to, what his life was like. What I don't like about the Epstein story is that while it's undeniably of some public interest, it's consumed an inordinate amount of time and attention, but Epstein is already dead. There is no bringing justice to this man. Some people's reputations are being very negatively affected by our learning they talked with Epstein in a friendly manner, but it doesn't seem like there's any smoking guns in these files that are going to lead to prison sentences. And the people who are taking the reputation hits mostly seem to be near the end of their lives anyway, like the 88-year-old Les Wexner, the 90-year-old Woody Allen, or the 97-year-old Noam Chomsky, who in 2023 suffered a stroke that left him unable to speak in his own defense. Bannon, 72, is already so generally despicable that it almost feels as though nothing's lost. The person who's so far paid the greatest price, in terms of public humiliation, is Prince Andrew, likely followed by Gates. It's not futile, but it's not exactly the grand retribution people were hoping for, either. And while all the spotlight is on Epstein's grave, do you know who's worse than Epstein? Tulsi Gabbard. Tulsi Gabbard does more harm to the world than Epstein. And unlike Epstein, Gabbard is still alive and kicking, still actively plotting to lie to the public and help Donald Trump further dismantle American democracy. And she's just one example. RFK Jr is worse than Epstein. He's done more harm to the world than Epstein has, he's harmed more people, he's getting people killed. Elon Musk is a worse person than Epstein. Pam Bondi is a worse person than Epstein. These are people involved in causing destruction and spreading suffering on a larger scale, and unlike with Epstein, there's no secret plot you need to uncover here. It's happening out in the open. There's something that feels off about how much more attention we're paying to a dead man's emails than to the real fires raging in front of us. As for Trump? I doubt there's going to be anything that comes out showing Trump was molesting children. Epstein did say he was Trump's best friend, but the impression I had was that this was probably a one-sided thing, a description Epstein would make that Trump wouldn't. Trump had no real friends, ergo Epstein, by being a friend at all, observed that he was effectively Trump's best friend. And maybe he benefited from that somehow. I don't know the exact nature of their relationship, and now we may never know. But much like everybody else involved in this, I doubt Trump is ever going to face criminal punishment for anything relating to Epstein. In the most immediate sense, the Epstein story is "good" because it may harm the Trump administration by reducing support for his government. It's better that people are bothered and turn against him than not care and keep supporting him no matter what. But it also seems absurd, surreal even, how many people could shrug at what's happening in America, at the lawless authoritarianism and corruption and centralization of power, but draw the line here, at the possibility of Trump victimizing girls decades ago. In the grand scheme of things it changes nothing. Trump should be removed from power and thrown in prison. If it turns out he also raped a kid decades ago, then he should be removed from power and thrown in prison for a thousand and one reasons instead of only a thousand. There is a generally exaggerated response to pedophilia, to use the term loosely (as sexual activity with girls younger than 18 or so). Is it worse than murder? Is it worse than committing war crimes? Is it worse than bombing a hospital? A child is harmed, but that is that really the height of human sin? I ask only because that's how many people seem to talk about it. Among all the very bad things a person could do, pedophilia seems to reign supreme in how strongly we react, and it's not even close. Judging by how the public reacts, you would think if Michael Jackson had indeed been sexual with that little boy, it's easily ten times worse than if he'd murdered the same boy instead. We're heavily disincentivized to unravel and investigate this oddity because people react by implying there's something suspicious about *not* sharing their over-the-top reaction to the act. And perhaps that dynamic of suspicion and intrigue is part of how the hatred for it is so performative and extreme: it's an attempt at demonstrating to us all that you must not be one yourself. How could you be, if you so clearly despise them? It reminds me of the original meaning of "homophobia": men being scared of being perceived as gay, who then express an even stronger hatred toward gay people as a result because they think it wards off suspicion. And just as we've seen vocally anti-gay people turn out to have been gay in secrecy, we've seen people warning us in strong terms about the danger of pedophiles (especially gay and trans pedophiles) coming for our children later get in trouble for engaging in those crimes theirselves. I'll always remember when Andrew Tate, asked about his own involvement in rape and sex trafficking, say he couldn't be a rapist because in the past he's said rapists should get the death penalty. *Surely* somebody can't be a rapist if they say rapists should get the death penalty! (Spoilers: he was a rapist.) It also gives people an acceptable place to channel what would otherwise be absurd violent tendencies. If I said I wanted to engage in vigilantism where I personally torture and execute somebody in public, I might be looked on like a psychopath, but if you rephrase this to be about wanting to torture and execute child molesters, you might disguise your bloodlust as an innocent passion for justice. However, when people are so enthusiastic to share their colourful fantasies of what they would inflict on a criminal, to me it almost always feels as though the penchant for violent retribution came first, and they've simply chosen a target that's socially acceptable. The pedophile represents the lowest of the low, the person nobody in society wants to stake their own status or reputation to defend, so you can say anything you like about them and expect to not be challenged. In the world of conspiracy theories, somewhere along the way, large portions of the public became obsessed and enamored with the concept of a secret pedophile ring. It used to be that we said the world was being run by powerful men who would retreat to shady back rooms and worship the devil. We were being manipulated by a cabal of satanists! Now, the devil-worship has been replaced with getting together and raping children. We're being manipulated by a cabal of pedophiles! It's something people have been trying to will into existence for a while now, and Epstein's case therefore greatly excited them because it represented this concept finally becoming reality. Surely pedophiles interacting with one another was bound to happen somewhere, but most people caught in FBI busts were far from ruling-the-world status. Epstein is probably the most well-connected person who's ever been deemed guilty of molesting kids. For years people wanted it to be George Soros, Hillary Clinton, Barack Obama, somebody at the World Economic Forum, or something like that. But Epstein will have to do.
