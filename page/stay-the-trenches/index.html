In 2014, being online and trying to use social media as a right-winger felt oppressive. It felt like all the big social media websites were dominated by left-wing sentiment, that the companies were all biased for the left, probably being run by left-wing employees, and it was easy to get banned or otherwise censored if you tried arguing or sharing a right-wing perspective, especially about any hot-button social issues like race or gender. Some right-wingers gave up and left. They said these platforms were bad, and they needed to create their own space as an alternative. For the most part, these attempts at creating alternative platforms weren't very successful. But a lot of other right-wingers stayed and fought. They kept making facebook accounts, they kept making twitter accounts, they kept making YouTube accounts, they kept posting videos and memes and comments. They felt like the platforms were against them, but they didn't go away. 10 years later, and the tides have shifted. Now many of these platforms are dominated by right-wing voices. Tech company leadership has also changed its tune. They must've realized right-wingers are a big part of their userbase. Now they're doing more to appeal to the right than to the left. In 2025, being online and using social media makes one feel like everything has been captured by the right, because it has. The Trump cult is everywhere. It's a wasteland of anti-American, anti-NATO, anti-Liberal, anti-Western, anti-science, pro-Russia misinformation, AI slop, and bot accounts hyping each other up as they try to convince you that all of this is normal. Elon Musk rotted his brain spending 10 hours a day staring into the void, then decided he needed to hijack the company for himself. He took it private, got rid of anyone whose job was to combat botting on the platform, stopped even pretending to be impartial about anything, and changed how it works to manipulate it into favouring his choice of narratives. Zuckerberg, meanwhile, downloaded new programming and decided that he supports Trump now, right around the time Trump was coming back into the White House, and he's even been ever so cooperatively lying about Biden-era "censorship" to help feed the right-wing waves that he's now riding. And Zuckerberg is far worse than Elon in most ways. Meta is a far worse company than X Corp in most ways. He just doesn't chase the limelight quite as much as Elon does, so he doesn't even get his fair share of the backlash. Facebook and Instagram are full of nonsense too, and they're bigger than Twitter. And they control WhatsApp. And Oculus. And Threads. And they have their own advertising network like Google does. It's really incredible how powerful this company is. No matter which website you're using, the rules have been rewritten. The algorithms have been tweaked. They amplify right-wing talking points now. They protect right-wing people and parties. And there's now 10 times more right-wing podcasts than you've ever even heard of. They all start one, even though all say the same shit. What do we do? Stop using these platforms? Go make our own? That's a tempting thought. I wish there was something nearly identical to these websites, but owned by better people. The difficult part is how you get everyone to stop using the current ones. When I open twitter.com, it seems like the whole website is garbage. It's rage-bait, crypto scams, half the people who get pushed are low-key Nazis, everyone's arguing about some contrived culture-war moment of the week that it's hard to believe anyone seriously cares about, but as a user I'm primed to have an opinion on, because it's being put in front of my face and I'm seeing everyone *else's* takes on it. It's a hellscape. And I don't have a site history to suggest this is what I wanted to see. This is basically the default Twitter, the one shown to anybody who makes an account. This is what Twitter itself *wants* to serve you. It's what they *wanted* you to see. When I look at Bluesky, it seems like an overall better experience, though that's probably partly because the userbase is much smaller, and it's not being targeted for manipulation as heavily as the larger platforms. Twitter reportedly has over 500 million monthly active users. Tumblr still has about 100 million. Bluesky is reportedly hovering around 20 million. I'm happy to use Bluesky anyway to contribute to its growth, because it seems like a better-designed platform run by better people, even before you get into the technical aspects of the way it works, with their AT Protocol that nobody understands, but which is apparently appealing to some people. It's not as feature-rich, but it's definitely a nice alternative that you should take seriously, and *maybe* as time goes by, it'll someday overtake other platforms. The problem is how such a large part of the value of a social media company is its network effect, that is, the number of other people who are using it. When you post onto Bluesky, it's like you're shouting into a crowd of 20 people; when you post onto Twitter, it's 500 people. If your goal is to reach a lot of people, Twitter has the bigger pool. And there's basically nothing Bluesky can offer that outdoes that raw size difference. Like the right-wingers of 2014, we probably shouldn't just abandon these platforms. It's possible the winds will change again, and eventually the dominant narrative on these platforms will shift, the way these companies are being run will change, they'll stop trying to appeal to the right, maybe some of them will even ban outright Nazis again, and maybe things will be different. But when that happens, you want to already have a presence in these places. In fact, your presence in these places can be part of what causes those shifts to happen. It might seem hopeless at the moment, because these platforms are controlled by private actors, private interests. In the case of Twitter, it's not even publicly traded. Elon Musk is allowed to completely manipulate it, even to the disadvantage of Twitter as a business. There's no shareholders that he's accountable to. And it doesn't seem like Elon himself is going to change course. He's an ideologue, and a rather proud, stubborn one at that time. But he could surprise us. It's not outside the realm of possibility. As for Zuckerberg, who knows. For how much attention they've both received, neither of these men are easy to predict. It's possible something could happen to these companies. Elon or Zuckerberg could randomly slip on a banana peel and die tomorrow, or two years from now, or ten, and then who knows what happens after they're gone. They could also get less active and move on as they get older, have a change of mind, take a step back. Bill Gates did. Then in their absence, more sane people who work at these companies could step up. Not becoming left-wing necessarily, but being more neutral, banning bots, allowing the platform to shift in a more normal direction. There are so many things that could happen to change the tides. And again, think of the situation the right was facing in 2014. It probably also felt somewhat hopeless. Silicon Valley, in the San Francisco Bay Area, where all these companies were centred, was seen as a home of progressive attitudes, which had permeated throughout all of these companies' internal culture, to the employees who worked there, the HR departments, everything seemed like it was oriented around progressive politics. And yet they continued trying to use these platforms and carving out a place for theirselves on them. It's hard to predict how things might turn out. So it can be unpleasant in the moment. I don't like browsing Twitter. It sucks. But this is why I'm not in full boycott of these places, and I still post on them, even though I try to also support alternatives growing. But I think it's better in the long run to not totally abandon and cede ground, and instead stay and have your voice be part of the website, and who knows, maybe one day things will change for the better.
