It's become commonplace in far-left communities to conflate Israel's very existence with extremism, using "Zionism" as though a nasty term for somebody with reprehensible views. The term "Zion" comes from the name of a hill in Jerusalem where Solomon's Temple was built in the Ancient Kingdom of Judah, used as a synecdoche for the whole region. In the late 19th century, some Jewish people grew weary of the persecution that had been such a common part of their historical experience, and thus began theorizing about the creation of their own state, a state for Jews, a place where Jews didn't have to accept being the powerless minority in some other group's country --- where, when the going gets tough, they were often on the chopping block. They wanted a home, a refuge, like Zion was in their lingering collective memory; and so the movement to create such a thing called itself Zionism. It was this movement that led to the creation of the modern country of Israel in the 1940s. So, what does Zionism today entail as a political belief? If anything, it may simply be that Israel ought to exist as a country where Jews lives, but not with any particular instruction for how that country ought to behave or be governed beyond that. --- It could be a repressive monarchy, like other Middle Eastern countries, or it could be progressive-minded libertarian socialists, as many early Zionists were. There's always been a plurality of political attitudes there. --- Nor is there any real Zionist *movement* to speak of, considering its only goal was achieved 78 years ago. It would be like speaking of Algerian separatists in modern-day Algeria, even though Algera already separated from France in the '60s. This is why "Zionism" has often been explained as merely "Jewish self-determination," and why anyone who believes in a "two-state solution" is considered a Zionist, regardless of their attitude about the conflict or their concern for Palestinians: by speaking of two states, they're implicitly allowing for one of the two states to be a Jewish one, rather than demanding that the Jews be reorganized into a single state where the Jewish population would be at the mercy of an Arab majority. That, ultimately, is the demand of "anti-Zionists." It's especially fashionable to compare Jews or Israelis (that is, the Jewish Israelis only) with Nazis.
