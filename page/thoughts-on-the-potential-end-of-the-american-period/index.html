!meta date: 2026-01-28 title: Thoughts on the potential end of the American period see-also: substack|thoughts-on-the-potential-end-of-the-american-period I used to say the US might not be a perfect country, but it's a bulwark against other countries that are clearly even worse. It at least has good values, good ideals, even where it doesn't always live up to them. It pushes things in the right direction. Therefore even if you give in to its worst critics and say the US is a bad country on net, it would still be the best of the bad countries, the least evil villain. And I do not, in fact, give in to those critics. The US is a net force for good in the world. It produces great media, scholarship, and technology. It has good, competent people in positions of leadership. It uses its military strength to uphold a world order that's beneficial for itself, but also for the rest of us. But these days America is no longer seeming like the country we once knew. Trump has spent the last year showing us his disdain for the truth, the rule of law, for all established norms of civil government, and for America's international commitments. This is a man who lost an election, plotted to have its results overturned, and used a mob of his supporters to pressure lawmakers into accepting that plot. It was the first time in America's history there was not a peaceful transfer of power. If America were the country we thought it was, that should've been the end of him. Instead, America failed to deliver justice on this man, and the public voted him back into office. Now the departments of the executive are being purged of all competent and principled people, to be replaced with obedient sycophants. The supreme court, compromised by repeated nomination of barely-qualified ideologue, is happy to stand by, if not actively aid, the slow march into authoritarianism. The American congress, with its Republican majority, is likewise choosing to stand by and do nothing as the president encroaches on what had been traditionally been the powers of the legislature. The pace of this decline has been overwhelming. It's no longer obvious if the US is a better power and ally than China. It might still be, but now it's a question. It's no longer obvious. There's doubt. And this doubt has serious political implications for the world. Think not only of Western Europe and the Anglosphere, but of all the middling and developing economies throughout the world that are looking for friends and investment. They must now consider the US may at any moment [torch its trading relations with you](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/economy/trump-sets-tariff-levels-reshapes-economy-rcna222387) and [call its own treaties irrelevant](https://www.cbc.ca/news/world/trump-says-cusma-irrelevant-canada-us-9.7044008), as it's done with Mexico and Canada. The US now openly threatens and questions the sovereignty of its [neighbours](https://www.nytimes.com/2025/03/07/world/canada/trump-trudeau-canada-51st-state.html) and [allies](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cy7mev35x2lo). And the US not only feels it has no obligation to international law, but now, as we've seen by [its dealings with Venezuela](https://www.cnn.com/2025/11/02/politics/timeline-us-strikes-caribbean-pacific-vis), grants itself the right to enforce its own laws in other countries, including by military invasion into your country to [capture your head of state](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cdred61epg4o) and smuggle him back to America to [stand trial in American courts](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/venezuelas-maduro-appear-us-court-trump-says-further-strikes-possible-2026-01-05/) for violating laws that don't exist in their own country. If a country had to choose between being aligned with either the US or China, the argument for choosing China has never been stronger. # The bad timeline Let's suppose there's no grand crescendo. If America doesn't get better and redeem itself, and remains on its current path indefinitely, what does that look like? Though some people speculate about his declining health, there's no guarantee Trump is going anywhere. He has access to the best medical care in the world and he's only turning 80 this year (his father made it to 93). He could be here for another 15 years for all we know. And maybe he'll annex Greenland and (some parts of) Canada before he leaves. Either way, the country after him will be very different from the one that first elected him. By the election of 2028, maybe voters won't have forgotten about all the lies and crime and corruption and the way Trump strangled the economy for his own self-enrichment. But they might not hold the Republican Party responsible for any of this. They could just move on to arguing about the next two candidates. The Republican frontrunner could simply disavow anything too unpopular Trump does and we'll go back to arguing about how the Democratic candidate is too woke and wants open borders and men in women's sports, or whatever the standard lines end up being by then. And maybe more than half of voters will agree, and the Republicans will get another four years in power. Will it be JD Vance? It could be, but it hardly matters. It will be somebody who supported Trump along the way, who won't fully disavow and condemn him, but who won't fill Trump's shoes personality-wise. They'll be more calm and measured, not blurting things out and making all the same blunders as Trump. And now that Trump has paved the way for them, they'll be free to carry on being as much of a dictator as they want. The country at this point is fully degraded, more like other pseudo-democracies like Turkey or Russia than the country it once was. # The optimistic timeline It's possible things do change. Americans might recognize and be horrified at what Trump and Republicans have done. They see their values trampled, their institutions gutted, their elections compromised. The Democratic Party comes back in full force. By 2029, there's a Democratic president and a Democratic majority in both chambers of Congress. Then what? They could begin cleaning up the mess Trump left behind, reversing his orders, rehiring all the competent people he purged, restoring key departments like the DOJ and HHS to working order. The American president will eventually give a speech in which they proclaim that America is back, and we all breathe a sigh of relief. But is that really the end? This has all happened before. Joe Biden was elected in 2021 and the Democrats got a majority in both chambers. They worked to repair what Trump had done. It felt good at first, but it didn't last. Trump and the MAGA culture that grew around him didn't go away, the Republican Party didn't get better, the Republican-aligned media sphere didn't go away. No lessons were learned, they didn't calm down or get better, they got worse, they got crazier, they doubled down on everything, and after a few years they came back in full force. The opposition taking back power was a good development, but it wasn't a good ending, because it *wasn't* an *ending*. To truly salvage America is going to require more than the Republican Party losing an election cycle and the Dems once again playing clean-up. To lose an election should not be thought of as a punishment; it's something that already happens every other election cycle anyway. What the country needs is actual justice, in the form of people being sent to prison, barred from office, companies and organizations being shut down. That or you need fundamental, structural change to how the government and elections work. Anything less than a serious overhaul and you should assume this is all going to happen again, because the cracks are still there, and the people who want to abuse those cracks are still here. For a time, stop trying to appease Republican voters. Trump supporters have no shame or principles and can never be satisfied. Now is not the time to seek unity with them and be a "president for all Americans". That's what Joe Biden attempted and look how well it worked out for him. They hate him. The sentiment of compromise is lost on them. Now is the time to take decisive action and save your country, even if that means Republicans need to be dragged kicking and screaming toward the finish line. No matter what you do or how you do it, they won't make this easy, so let them be difficult, let them make this difficult, but get it done. ## Justice - **Government**: - Donald Trump, his sons, and any other person conspiring with him to enrich theirselves through the presidency (e.g. the Witkoff family), all need to be thrown in prison. There should be a way for the opposition leader to introduce legislation for a vote without needing a discharge petition. - The Senate granting exactly 2 seats to each state regardless of population is absurd. There should be some kind of weighting or consideration for size. The exact details are entirely negotiable. It could be as simple as the ten largest states getting a third senator and the ten smallest only getting one. It doesn't matter. The Senate in its current form systematically grants the Republican Party disproportionate power over Congress for no reason, thus empowering its fringe elements and allowing it to be less moderate than would be necessary (for it to win elections) otherwise. - **Presidency**: - The president should be elected directly through a popular vote across the country. The electoral college system that exists today should be abolished. This is important not only because it eliminates the possibility for the candidate who loses the popular vote to be installed as president (e.g. Trump in 2016), but because it means the presidential race would no longer fixate on winning battleground states or swing states, with each candidate tailoring their campaigns and platforms around only those areas. In 2024 the national conversation focused more on the needs and priorities of Michigan and Pennsylvania than California, New York, Texas, and Florida. This is absurd and needs to change. The country's leadership should not be set every four years by the most-divided states. - The US president's pardon power should be abolished. This is a holdover from the days of monarchy and the norm of its use has been corrupted. If a pardon power as we now understand it should exist at all, perhaps have it be a power of Congress. - It's far too difficult to impeach and remove a president. Try lowering the requirement from two thirds of the Senate to three fifths (from 67 to 60). - Take a note from parliamentary govenrments: introduce a new removal mechanism that doesn't require an implicit admission of guilt to high crimes, which can be activated either by a majority of the House and three fifths of the Senate *or* by a majority of the president's own party in both chambers. - **Supreme Court**: - The supreme court is too powerful and ideologically compromised. It should undergo significant reform. I leave the exact best way to do this for others. There are many ideas floating about, and I'm unsure which would be most effective. But the court's current form is largely incidental and cannot be treated as a sacred thing that must be preserved. On the contrary, we should rather assume that *something* must change. - The introduction of an enforceable supreme court code of ethics, as Joe Biden was proposing, and the introduction of term limits, are simple places to start. - There should also probably be an attempt at impeaching current justices for their conduct, i.e. Alito and Thomas. - The size of the supreme court could be increased, there could be multiple rosters of high judges instead of all nine hearing every case, and so on. There is no noteworthy proposal for supreme court reform I know of that should not at least be on the table for consideration in future discussions of this matter. - **Other**: - The US needs to attempt repairing its relations with Western allies and immediately rejoin international commitments. The status of the United States in these commitments must also be codified in such a way that makes it clear this is not for the president to decide unilaterally in the way Trump has done. - The Immigration and Customs Enforcement agency (I.C.E.), to whatever extent it's still around, should have all its offices shuttered, all its current leadership put under investigation, and anybody who was involved in its leadership during Trump's term should be barred from working in public sector positions ever again. Identify and track down whatever Nazis are running the social media accounts and publish all the findings publicly, because this is an accountability crisis as it stands. - Start a committee to investigate what happened with Elon Musk's D.O.G.E. and any of the people or companies connected to it. Who got access to what data and what happened to it? It's unacceptable that this passed and everybody simply moved on. Any remnants of this program that may still be in existence can be obviously be thrown out immediately. - **Other US territories**: - Puerto Rico should receive statehood, becoming the 51st. If it were a state, it would be 33rd by population, making it larger than two fifths of existing states (examples of states smaller than it: Mississippi, New Mexico, Arkansas, Vermont, Delaware). It's absurd that America rules over such a large population but gives it no voice in the US presidential race nor any representation in Congress. - There's a reasonable argument to make Washington, D.C. a state as well. It's not as large as Puerto Rico, but it would still be larger by population than Vermont or Wyoming (making it the 3rd smallest), and has hundreds of thousands of residents who are currently disenfranchised for no reason, as they can't vote in any state elections and have no representation in Congress (though they do get some electoral votes in the presidential race). - It's argued that D.C. is meant to be a neutral meeting place for the federal government where no state government has authority to ensure that no state has undue influence over the union, but this fear isn't realistic and doesn't justify D.C. have no federal representation. There are many countries where the capital city is simply part of the country in the same way any other city is (e.g. London, Paris, Berlin, Athens, Moscow, Stockholm, Ottawa) and it doesn't lead to a power struggle between the federal government and regional powers. - There are countries where their capital is a separate federal district, such as Brazil and its capital city Brasília; but while Brasília is not formally called a state and is not governed in the same way as other Brazilian states are, its residents still elect representatives to the federal assembly as states do, meaning they're not disenfranchised like D.C. residents. So, it's possible we don't formally declare D.C. a state and give it a star on the nation's flag, and it could still be a special district where the federal government has more direct municipal policing powers at will---but considering it's a distinct population that doesn't get to vote in the elections of any other state, it should be given a House seat and two Senators, the way a small state would've been. - There is not as strong a case for granting statehood to the remaining low-population US territories (Guam, American Samoa, the Northern Mariana Islands, and the US Virgin Islands). However, the residents of these areas should get to vote in the presidential race. # What led to this? When a country becomes polarized against itself, some people will begin to resent their neighbours, and that resentment may grow to overpower their belief in what used to be uniting principles. In America, this happened among their otherwise Republican-voting population. They were propagandized over decades to hate the Democrats, immigrants, the black population, the abortion doctors, and the woke progressive gender-bendering queers, gays, and transgenders, and eventually their hatred for all of these enemies exceeded their belief in democracy, the rule of law, and the constitution, which went from feeling like virtues to feeling like contraints that protect the wrong sort of people. They couldn't yet articulate it, but they had become hungry for a dictator, for a hired gun to come smite their enemies. Even before Trump appeared before them, they had a Trump-shaped hole in their heart. Look at how Trump and his movement function. The people are resentful but don't know where to direct their resentment, and Trump is their leader only insofar as he points them in a direction. If you listen, Trump will tell you which countries are ripping us off, he tells you which immigrant groups to be mad at. He tells you the Democrats and the judges are all radicals who want to destroy the country. From the very beginning Trump's campaign was all about target selection. Mario Rubio, of all people, [keenly described the phenomenon](https://www.vox.com/2016/3/14/11218948/trump-rubio-violence-rally) years ago, speaking after Trump had encouraged his supporters to beat up protesters, offering to pay their legal fees if they did. Rubio, in 2016: "This has happened repeatedly now. This is not new. This is a pattern of the idea that: We are angry. And since we are angry we can say or do whatever we want. "This is the intentional injection of the use of people's anger, basically. This is a political candidate in Donald Trump who has identified that there's some really angry people in America. "And along comes a presidential candidate and says to you, 'You know why your life is hard? Because fill in the blank---somebody, someone, some country---they're the reasons for it. Give me power, so I can go after them.' That's what he's feeding into." Now Trump's implied promise is more than legal fees. There are people committing crimes on Trump's behalf and refusing to talk because they believe Trump will bail them out. And he does. This is the incentive structure Trump creates: that if you break the rules on his behalf, he'll protect you. And this, too, has been a quality of his campaign from the very beginning. In this sense Trump is fighting a sort of spiritual civil war. The new norm under his government is that he's not a president for all Americans, but for his supporters, for Republican-led states that support him and coöperate with his government and its agenda. Democrats should meanwhile be mistreated and fought and punished simply because they're the opposition. That's why I.C.E. is in Minnesota right now. Trump and his followers want violence and destruction to befall Minnesota because they view it as something akin to an enemy nation. # Why was America so susceptible? America has been undergoing social, economic, and demographic change, which leads to trouble in other countries too. And in other countries, the same phenomenon emerges: a cohort of people who are so resentful they're willing to abandon their uniting principles to fight an internal enemy. In Germany, support for the AfD is similar to support for Trump, but they haven't succeeded at seizing power. To some extent this is because of America's faulty political system, which gives disproportionate power to certain areas of the country. We could also pay attention to how much American politics has come to revolve around a small number of sticky, divisive cultural issues, which play nowhere near as large a part of party loyalty in other places. In the view of many Americans, the most important questions for the nation are guns, abortion, and the status of gay and trans people. The Republican Party can do virtually anything it likes and these people will *never* vote for the Democrats instead because they feel the Democrats are going to come and take their guns, let women kill their own babies, and show every first-grader in school graphic pornography while encouraging them to be gay or change their gender identity. And over the last decade or so there's been a new entry to this list of cardinal grievances: the idea there's a hoard of thieves, rapists, and drug dealers waiting at the American border, and the Democrats want to open the floodgates and let them pour into your neighbourhood unchecked. And if you think there's anything wrong with that, they'll call you a racist and ban you from the internet. These delusional fears, concerning things of an exaggerated important to begin with, keep the people in line without the Republican Party needing to really do anything. And it's all regularly affirmed by the right-wing media sphere, whose main goal is maintaining this voter loyalty and fear of the other. In conversation with Americans, it's difficult to say if they realize how different the rest of the world is on this front. In the two decades where I've lived in numerous places across Canada, I've never heard people arguing about guns, gays, god, or abortion. Try listening to our leadership debates before an election. Politicians don't talk about these topics and if they did it would be strange because most people don't care. The party divide here is mostly a reflection of different attitudes about spending, taxation, infrastructure and social services. In the last decade or so immigration has become a popular issue, but the parties don't have a radically different view of the problem, nor do you hear the same vulgar implications about the topic as are now common in American discourse (e.g. that our more progressive parties are trying to pursue "open borders" in order to facilitate a mass invasion of the country as a desperate ploy to gain immigrant voters). Even when we talk about the same issues, we do so in a less crazy, less divisive manner. And though it's not American-specific, another major catalyst for political change across the world has been the internet media revolution and how the average person has reacted to it. ## The internet media revolution Say I go to a supermarket or pharmacy. I don't need to worry much about whether the food or drugs on the shelves are safe. I can buy things with confidence because I know I'm within a system that has rules and regulators every step of the way to ensure this is the case. Imagine what it would be like if all our regulators and quality assurance disappeared, new stores opened up selling things that hadn't been tested, and the possibility of food or drugs at the market being harmful to you skyrockets. What would be the result? If we couldn't implement regulation of some kind, consumers would need to change their behaviour. We would need to become more skeptical shoppers, doing more work to distinguish good from bad product, or to find reputable vendors. We could no longer be as free or careless as we once were. A similar thing happened to our media. It used to be that if you heard a claim on the news or read it in an article by a reputable paper, you could safely assume it was true. They would never fabricate things, they probably made a reasonable attempt to verify it, and they probably even made an effort to look into the topic a bit to present it fairly, instead of uncritically blurting out the first claim they came across. This is still true today when interacting with traditional, reputable media companies. But when the internet started displacing traditional media as the way people got their news, the quality of the product changed. The standards dropped. There were suddenly many more sources of information, without the experience nor the same checks on what they were putting out. So, in response, people needd to change their behaviour. We had to become more media literate, more skeptical viewers, doing more work to distinguish good from bad reporting, to find reputable outlets and writers and commentators. But this didn't happen, at least not for enough of us. In general society has failed to adapt to these changes circumstances. Too many people still automatically believe what they see as though it were a trustworthy article by a reputable source, even if it's just some random person with a YouTube channel or an image of text you scrolled by on Facebook or Twitter. It's like we're living through a transitional age where everybody is constantly getting sick from their food and drugs because we're still buying things from the store carelessly as though it were still well-regulated, when it no longer is. There's been an internet media revolution, and it's ruined people's ability to navigate and understand the world around them. A lesson to be learned from the last decade or two is that the fabled "marketplace of ideas" doesn't work as intended, that is, it doesn't work through free association alone, without institutions and rules and enforcement. The theory was if you let anybody say whatever they like, the people who lie and mislead will end up with a bad reputation and people will stop listening to them. The good quality product, that's accurate and reliable, will then rise to the top. This hasn't happened. Instead, the liars are successful at lying, and deceive millions of people regularly. Their dishonesty, inaccuracy, and unreliability doesn't produce the foretold bad reputation that causes people to stop listening to them. This is most clear when examining political figures like Trump, or media figures who have repeatedly been shown to be liars or to be financially compromised, like Tucker Carlson, Candace Owens, Elon Musk, Tim Pool, Hasan Piker, Alex Jones, and so on. If the marketplace of ideas worked as advertised, nobody would believe any of these people when they made a claim about anything, at least not until verified by some other, more reliable source. Their words should fall on deaf ears, they should be struggling to get anybody to pay them any attention at all. Instead they have large audiences, and no matter how many times they've lied before, every new lie they come up with or hear and repeat is taken seriously and believed by millions of people, and must therefore be addressed and disproved as though novel. # What should other countries do about America's decline? I'd love to be optimistic, but Americans must understand that we can no longer bet all our money on their waking from their stupor. Even if America seems to regain its sanity and wants to be our friend again, it will for a long time feel like there's a chance of it reverting to a Trump-style regime each election cycle. For America, we now feel a similar skepticism as we do for developing countries that appear to be heading in the right direction, but which still have problematic elements of their political culture that give us pause. Turkey was like this a few decades ago, but then, as feared, became too internally polarized, underwent democratic backsliding, and fell under control of Erdoğan. Their path was rather similar to the one America is on today. The West needs to reduce its dependence on America, first in matters of military. European countries have their own military forces, but they're still largely dependent on the support, logistics, and infrastructure of the American one, which it has until now ever so generously shared. This needs to change. Unfortunately, this means we need to make significant military investment, to a point where it's possible to operate independently from American support, which we can no longer trust will always be there for us. The other major area I find concerning is technology, especially communications. Try looking up a list of the most-used websites, apps, and services used globally, and you'll see a lot of American flags. Google, YouTube, Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, WhatsApp, Messenger, Reddit, Pinterest, Bing, Discord, Quora, Amazon, Netflix, Yahoo, all the popular AI clients like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Grok, it's all headquartered in America, owned by American companies and beholden to the American government. We should not be comfortable with this. It's an issue more obvious now than ever before that politicians in other countries are dependent on using American-owned websites to communicate with their own constituents. It's easier said than done and it may feel like there's a long road ahead of us, but we really need to start investing in non-American tech companies. I take no pleasure in America's downfall. I wish it didn't have to be this way. If America were to remain good and strong, it would make things easier for everybody. But this is where we've come to. .I'm against the death penalty in all cases. No reference I make to treason or to people being traitors is ever intended to imply they should be put to death. I don't believe treason should be punished with death. I believe traitors should be punished with humane prison sentences.
