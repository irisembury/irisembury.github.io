The most obvious difference in the two versions I have is the pattern on the wall behind her, but many other small details differ. ||image-span 200 21-6.png Interestingly, if you try and determine its identity, there are two common answers: * **A Beauty Holding a Bird** by Louis Emile Pinel de Grandchamp (1831--1894) * **L'oiseau Chéri** ('The Beloved Bird') by Alexandre Cabanel (1823--1889) Cabanel is the more well-known artist. Even if you don't recognize his name, you'd probably recognize many of his paintings, like [The Birth of Venus](The Birth of Venus (1863\) - Alexandre Cabanel.png)[](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sandro_Botticelli_046.jpg) or [Nymph abducted by a faun](media/Nymph abducted by a faun (1860\) - Alexandre Cabanel.png)[](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nymphe_et_Saty_(Alexandre_Cabanel\)_1860.JPG). Grandchamp doesn't even have a Wikipedia page. What a nobody! But you can find his other works on various art websites, and his name is mentioned on some index pages, such as this [list of Orientialist artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Orientalist_artists) on Wikipedia (his name is included but doesn't link to anything), meaning Western artists who did works with Arabic or Asian influence, as this work does appear to be. If you look at other paintings by Cabanel, they don't resemble this one in theme or style. Cabanel was more interested in classical European style and specialized in portraiture and European myth. Whereas Pinel de Grandchamp's other work was more focused on slice of life and more often featured foreign cultures. I also feel Cabanel being a more noteworthy painter means if it were his, it would be easier for me to verify that with a higher degree of certainty. The fact there's a dispute about its origin adds plausibility to it being a painting by somebody lesser known, occasionally mistakenly attributed to somebody else. So I list this file as *A Beauty Holding a Bird* by Louis Emile Pinel de Grandchamp, not by Cabanel.
