I've been using it and edits of it across the internet for a long time now, often as a logo or profile picture. Then at some point I realized there were different versions of it, credited to different people. I tracked down the best quality version of each: !gallery media/A Beauty Holding a Bird (n.d.) - Louis-Emile Pinel de Grandchamp.png | **A Beauty Holding a Bird** by Louis-Emile Pinel de Grandchamp (1831--1894) media/Alexandre Cabanel - L'oiseau Chéri.png | **L'oiseau Chéri** ('The Beloved Bird') by Alexandre Cabanel (1823--1889) Aside from the colours being different, it's from a slightly different angle, in one version her hand comes up higher compared with the window, the pattern on the wall behind her is different, and other small details. It's impressive how well the hand, fabric, jewellery, and other details are emulated. But which is the original? Cabanel is a more well-known artist generally. If you don't recognize his name, you might still recognize many of his paintings, like [The Birth of Venus](media/The Birth of Venus (1863\) - Alexandre Cabanel.png)[](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sandro_Botticelli_046.jpg) or [Nymph abducted by a faun](media/Nymph abducted by a faun (1860\) - Alexandre Cabanel.png)[](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Nymphe_et_Saty_(Alexandre_Cabanel\)_1860.JPG). Grandchamp doesn't even have a Wikipedia page --- what a nobody! --- but you can find his work in many places, and, though it doesn't link to anything, his name is mentioned on Wikipedia's [list of Orientialist artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Orientalist_artists), meaning Western artists who did works with Arabic or Asian influence, as this work does appear to be. If you look at other paintings by Cabanel, they don't resemble this one in theme or style. Cabanel was more interested in classical European style and specialized in portraiture and European myth. Whereas Pinel de Grandchamp's other work was more focused on slice of life and more often featured foreign cultures. I also don't see a signature on the version credited to Grandchamp whereas Cabanel's version is signed (above the jewellery box), which would make sense if he were trying to make it clear that this was his reproduction, not the original. It's also possible the painting was originally by Grandchamp and then Cabanel painted his own version of it, as the two were contemporaries. This would neatly explain why there are two versions. --- # On the Seashore This is another painting I've used as a profile picture across some websites. I apprecated its form despite the very drab colours, though I'm not sure if this version was the original. I made a few edits of it, adding colour and then also expanding the shoreline with clone brush nonsense. If you look up the artist, George Elgar Hicks, on Wikipedia, there's a different version of this painting that I believe is not the original, as [on Wikimedia it's listed as a reproduction](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:George_elgar_hicks27.jpg). Its title is also listed as "Girl Seated by the Seashore," which doesn't seem right. The version I'm using can be found at ARC, the Art Renewal Center (an American non-profit), listed by the name ["On the Seashore"](https://www.artrenewal.org/artworks/on-the-seashore/george-elgar-hicks/41665), which is how I reference it. !square-gallery { "src":"media/On the Seashore (1878) - George Elgar Hicks.png", "caption":"The first version I found and started with" } { "src":"media/Girl Seated by the Seashore (1878) - George Elgar Hicks.jpg", "caption":"A reproduction hosted on Wikipedia" } { "src":"media/On the Seashore (1878) - George Elgar Hicks - changed hues.png", "caption":"I brightened it, rotated the colours" } { "src":"media/On the Seashore (1878) - George Elgar Hicks - changed hues, vignette.png", "caption":"Expanded, increased contrast, added vignette" } --- # In a vintage tavern !image-float media/In a vintage tavern (photo by Todd Diemer for Unsplash).jpg | The full original photo by Unsplash I randomly this picture one day while searching through Wikimedia commons.
